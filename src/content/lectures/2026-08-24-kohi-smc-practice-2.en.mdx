## The order of work

Tables and figures came first, and the text was only allowed to say what they showed. I compared it to delegating to a staff member: you set the direction and you check what comes back. The literature library and Introduction from part 1 went straight onto the front of the manuscript.

The practice dataset was synthetic: predicting diabetes from health-checkup data, 320 people and 18 variables (blood pressure, fasting glucose, HbA1c, lipids, smoking, alcohol, exercise), with diabetes status as the outcome. I said up front that it must not be cited as real findings.

## Six steps

1. **Checking the data.** Without telling participants what to look for, we ran `/clean-data`. Four problems were planted: a fasting glucose of 999, smoking coded as both "NEVER" and "never," a weight of 6.5 kg for a height of 171 cm, and blank cells. People decided how to handle each one, and the AI kept the record.
2. **Table 1.** Built with `/analyze-stats`, without letting the AI pick the tests on its own. The second checkpoint was picking two numbers from the table and checking them against the raw data. In the multivariable regression we read together how the confidence interval for BMI crossed 1.
3. **Figures.** `/make-figures` drew ROC curves for fasting glucose, HbA1c, and the combined model on one plot. We also covered calibration plots, the participant flow diagram, and common figure mistakes.
4. **Writing.** `/write-paper` drafted Methods from the cleaning record and Results using only the numbers in the table, and participants checked each number in the text against the table. We then went through a four-paragraph Discussion, the limitations paragraph, and a `/humanize` pass that leaves numbers untouched.
5. **Citations and journals.** `/verify-refs` checked the citations and `/check-reporting` compared the draft with the reporting guideline. Zotero inserted the references, and `/find-journal` suggested three journals with reasons.
6. **Slides and abstract.** `/present-paper` turned the same evidence into a 15-minute journal-club deck with speaker notes, and the slide numbers were checked against the table. The abstract came last.

## Something that happened while preparing

While preparing the material I verified 13 DOIs. All 13 resolved, but 5 pointed to a different paper. I used this to show why checking that a DOI opens is not enough.

## Closing

The last slides were about the difference between using AI well and leaning on it, which comes down to whether you can verify what it produces. For homework I suggested running the same sequence on their own data and growing their library. Thanks to everyone who came to both sessions, and to the organizers.
